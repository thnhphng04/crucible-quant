# ADR-0032 — The perpetual account is replayed host-side

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Task:** P3-05 … P3-08 · **Arch:** §3.4, §3.5, §6.1 · **Erodes:** P5

## Context

Moving from spot cash to Binance USDT-M perpetuals adds three mechanics the previous venue did not have: margin drawn from tiered leverage brackets, funding settled every eight hours, and liquidation on a mark price that is not the trade price. Hedge mode adds a fourth: one contract holds a LONG and a SHORT position at the same time, each with its **own** isolated margin and its own liquidation price.

The architecture's §3.5 answer to "how do we model execution" has always been NautilusTrader, under hard invariant P5: backtest and live run the same code path. So the first question was whether Nautilus can carry these four. The installed source was read rather than the architecture's snippets, per `CLAUDE.md`:

- `MarginAccount._margins` is a `cdef dict` keyed by `InstrumentId` alone (`accounting/accounts/margin.pxd:34`). One symbol cannot hold two isolated wallets. This is not a gap in the API; it is the data structure.
- `MarginModel.calculate_margin_init(instrument, quantity, price, leverage, use_quote_for_inverse)` has **no side parameter** (`accounting/margin_models.pxd:27-34`). Only `calculate_margin_maint` takes a `PositionSide`. A plugged-in margin model cannot see the side it is pricing.
- `OmsType.HEDGING` mints a **fresh `PositionId` per fill** (`execution/engine.pyx:1513`), not two fixed slots per symbol the way the venue does.
- The backtest engine settles no funding at all.

So "build it on Nautilus" would mean writing a margin model blind to the side, hand-assigning position IDs to fake two slots, reaching into the account balance from a `SimulationModule` to settle funding, and writing liquidation from scratch — that is, implementing essentially all of Binance's semantics while fighting an account object whose keying contradicts them.

`execution/engine._summarize` already reconstructs equity host-side from fills under a cash account. The shape exists; the question is only how far it extends.

## Decision

1. **Nautilus generates orders and fills; the account is ours.** The bridge keeps the signal → order → fill path, including ADR-0003's next-open convention and `LatencyModel(base_latency_nanos=1)`. Margin, funding and liquidation are replayed host-side in `execution/perp_account.py` and `execution/joint_account.py`.
2. **`OmsType.NETTING` with `AccountType.MARGIN`, not `HEDGING`.** One backtest runs one strategy on one side of one instrument, so that engine never holds both legs; the two-sided book belongs to the joint-account replay. `HEDGING` was tried first and actively harmful: a fresh `PositionId` per entry order left `reduce_only` stops with nothing to reduce and **silently stopped them filling**, caught only because `test_protective_stop_fills_at_gap_open` went red.
3. **The venue's own `margin_init` and `margin_maint` are zero.** Nautilus must not reject an order on numbers we do not use. The real bound is the bracket table.
4. **Leverage brackets are a locked snapshot, recorded as an assumption.** `GET /fapi/v1/leverageBracket` is signed and Binance publishes no history, so a campaign fetches one snapshot, hashes it into the lock and applies it to the whole in-sample window. Gate ⑥′ carries a sensitivity branch over brackets, alongside the existing `cost_multiplier` stress.
5. **Isolated means isolated, enforced at the wallet.** A wallet's loss stops at its own margin: `Wallet.bounded_pnl` bounds realized PnL at `−margin` and `Wallet.value` floors its equity contribution at zero. Neither the free balance nor the other side can be reached by one wallet's loss. A fill *through* the bankruptcy price is recorded as a **liquidation**, not a fill, because that is what the venue would have done.
6. **Intrabar resolution travels as a monotone path summary, never as minute bars.** The time-ordered sequence of running-minimum and running-maximum breakpoints is a **sufficient statistic for first touch** of any level in either direction. It also makes the ambiguity rule exact: a stop and a liquidation first reached in the same minute are genuinely unordered, and the summary says so rather than handing back a guess. **Corrected in P3-16:** this decision first claimed the summary saves "~1,200× the data". That number compared 1,440 minutes to *one daily bar*, not to the summary, and it was wrong as a justification. Measured over 900 simulated 1,440-minute paths, a day compresses to **~87 breakpoints** (p95 132; a strongly trending day pushes one side to 200–400) — **~16×** against raw minute rows and ~60–90× against full minute OHLCV, or ~1.6 MB per instrument-series over 1,840 bars. The real argument is not payload size but exact ambiguity semantics, plus the download and storage cost across the universe × two series × every campaign. The summary is built from each minute's **own low and high**, not its close: a minute is a bar, and its wick is exactly what reaches a level first. The module lives in `core/path_summary.py` (P3-16) because summaries are built at fetch time and `data` may not import `execution`.
6b. **A bar is cut into segments at its funding settlements, and each segment carries its own summary** (P3-16). Funding leaves the isolated wallet at fixed times, which moves the liquidation price *inside* the bar, and one monotone sequence cannot answer a level that changes part-way through: `[100, 80, 110, 100, 90]` and `[100, 80, 110, 100, 100]` have identical whole-bar summaries, yet a threshold of 95 coming into force after minute 3 is reached by the first and not the second. So `resolve_bar` takes one liquidation price **per segment**; a length mismatch is refused rather than reusing the last, because that would make a missed settlement invisible. A missing minute is refused too (INV-94): filling the gap yields an exact-looking answer to a question the data cannot answer.
7. **The per-bar replay order is fixed and hashed into the campaign protocol** as `PROTOCOL["replay"]`: funding, then liquidation on the mark, then the bar's fills with **closes before opens**. Funding first because paying it leaves the isolated wallet and so can be what tips a position over. Closes before opens because a slot must free its margin before another asks for it — and which slot that is has nothing to do with the alphabet, which is how sorting by instrument first silently denied funded entries.
8. **Fills outside the replay window are refused, not clamped.** Folding a fill from beyond the last bar into the last bar let a future trade move equity inside the measured period.
9. **Admission runs on one equity snapshot in canonical order** — instrument ascending, long before short — with the 10% portfolio cap and at most 10 positions, per ADR-0031 decision 6. `_SIDE_ORDER` is a literal rather than derived from the string, because the protocol hashes the ordering.
10. **The 25% clearance rule is kept and tested where it binds.** Liquidation must sit at least 25% of the entry-to-stop distance beyond the stop. At leverage 1 this is close to vacuous — liquidation is roughly a whole entry move away, far outside any few-ATR stop — so `assert_clearance` is tested against a **constructed** binding case. A rule that is vacuously satisfied in every test is not a rule.

## Consequences

- **P5 is eroded, and this is the price of the decision.** Backtest ≡ live still holds for signal → order → fill. It does **not** hold for margin, funding or liquidation: that arithmetic is ours. It must be reconciled against Binance testnet or a real statement before any capital is at risk. This is an obligation on whoever opens the first live campaign, not a footnote.
- **Two sources of truth for equity now exist** — Nautilus's account inside a single-strategy backtest, and `PerpAccount` inside the joint replay. They agree only because the single-strategy venue's margin is zeroed. Anyone re-enabling venue margin must reconcile both or pick one.
- **The joint replay is where the portfolio actually lives.** `validation.portfolio.combine` — N self-financed return streams weighted together — stops describing the real thing once ten strategies share one balance, one margin pool and one kill switch. The replay's slot contributions reconcile to account equity (INV-92b), which is what makes a per-instrument chart a decomposition rather than an invented subaccount.
- **The bracket snapshot is a dated assumption inside every result.** A campaign's margin arithmetic is only as good as the day its brackets were fetched, and Binance changes them. The lock records which day.
- **Nothing in the gate pipeline calls any of this yet.** The sandbox payload still carries only trade bars, so a backtest inside the container cannot receive mark prices or funding. The components are built and tested; the wiring is not. The Phase-3 gate is not met, and the roadmap records that rather than marking the tasks done.

## Alternatives considered

- **Implement it inside Nautilus anyway** — a side-blind margin model, hand-assigned position IDs to simulate two slots, a `SimulationModule` reaching into the balance for funding. Rejected: it is ~100% of the venue's semantics written against an account object keyed in a way that contradicts them, and it buys a P5 that is nominal rather than real, since the arithmetic would still be ours.
- **Model hedge mode as two netting sub-accounts inside Nautilus.** Rejected for the same keying reason, and because it splits the shared USDT balance that the whole phase exists to model.
- **Ship minute bars into the sandbox.** Rejected on measured cost (decision 6) and because it would not have made the ambiguity rule any more exact than the summary does.
- **Let a wallet's loss reach the free balance and rely on liquidation to prevent it.** Rejected: liquidation is checked on the mark, and a trade price can gap past bankruptcy between marks. The bound has to be at the wallet or it is not a bound.
